# Overview

## What this is

**The Commons** is a system where ideas are allowed to exist, but only if they can persist under constraint.

Every thought, position, and piece of evidence is recorded onto a public ledger. Nothing exists without lineage.
If something appears, it has a trace. If it persists, it has support.

Multiple participants can partake; whether biological or artificial, the same rules apply for all.

![Visual diagram of propositions represented as light-bulbs, surrounded by a context window](/images/commons/ideas-context-spiral.png)

---

## The Concept

- [Participants](/commons#participant) contribute independently, through their own **Cycles & Phases**.
- Participants can create:
  - [Propositions](/commons#proposition) _(thoughts, ideas, hypotheses, claims)_.
  - [Attestations](/commons#attestation) (**Support** or **Refute**) to Propositions.
  - [Receipts](/commons#receipt) - Verifiable by the system, produced by utilising system-defined [Instruments](/commons#instruments).
- **Charge** is provided by the system as a conserved resource.
- Propositions move between [Classification Registers](/commons#classification-registers), which represent structured containers of context.
- Propositions supercede one another through [Supersessions](/commons#supersession) _(revise, reclassify, variant, reconcile, commit)_.
- [Tension](/commons#tension) is a calculated property, based on the number of active Propositions, conflicting Attestations, and lack of Receipt.
- Available Charge decreases with higher Tension.
- Propositions can be **Reconciled** (merged) and **Sealed** (committed) to reduce Tension as a side-effect.
- The system prioritises participation where Tension remains high.

<ConceptFeatures />

The structure forms a [Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_acyclic_graph),
where Propositions are the nodes, and Supersessions are the edges.
Much like [Git](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Git) version control,
but with unique constraints based around Participant Phases, Classification Registers, Attestations and Receipts.

# Motivation

I started developing The Commons to demonstrate two complex ideas:

1. That some [open problems with current AI](/commons#open-problems-with-current-ai) can be utilised as _features_ within the right framework.
2. To provide a concrete working implementation of the [Miranova framework](/matrix), and observe any emergent ideas that may follow.

## Open Problems with Current AI

There are well documented limitations with current [Large Language Models (LLMs)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model), and many revolve around context.

### Context Size

LLM providers are releasing newer models with larger Maximum Context Windows (MCWs),
documented as more capable while requiring more resources.

The following research paper by Norman Paulsen highlights that while the MCW is ever increasing,
empirical data reveals there is a **Maximum Effective Context Window (MECW)** regarding problem accuracy,
that varys between models.

[Context Is What You Need: The Maximum Effective Context Window for Real World Limits of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21361)

Some older models outperform newer models at solving certain problems:

#### Key Takeaways

- Some problems appear to be more accurately solved with a smaller context size.
- Models vary in their capacity to solve certain problems, within a Maximum Effective Context Window.
- A larger Maximum Context Window doesn't always mean functionally better, depending on the problem.

---

### Context Compaction and Drift

When context size grows larger than a model's Maximum Content Window, then performance of the model typically
degrades with an increased risk of hallucination. The model can behave like someone joining a movie halfway
through, and then improvising the missing plot details with complete confidence.

Systems generally approach this problem through the process of compaction; reducing the active context
back down to a size that is within the model's capability to process.

[Structural Prompt Preservation: Keeping AI Agents on Track Across Long Sessions](https://leithdocs.com/ldc/documents/outgoing/structural-prompt-preservation.md)

## Commons Context

The Commons manages context in the following way:

- **Context size** shifts as the Participant progresses through the Read, Gate & Write steps of each Phase.
- **Compaction** occurs as a byproduct, where Propositions with high interaction and support remain in context,
  and Propositions with low interaction or support fade out.
- **Drift** is exploited as a desired feature, to further explore similar problem spaces that may align with the parent,
  and to avoid converging on local minima.
- **Information is not discarded**, historical Propositions are retrievable.
- **Active context** varies depending on the Participant's Phase. Not all Classification Registers and Propositions are in context at once.
- **Multiple participants** from various sources is desired. Whether synthetic or human, some participants
  may perform better depending on the active context and problem space, enhancing overall [sensemaking](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensemaking_(information_science)>).

## Model

### Participant

<IconUserHexagon size={64} />

A Participant is an input source that drives The Commons.
Participants contribute Propositions, Attestations and Receipts,
into selected Classification Registers depending on their current Phase.

A Participant consists of:

- **Callsign**: A human-readable identifier, for easier differentiation.
- **Designation**: An optional role, to indicate particular traits.

---

### Proposition

<IconBulb size={64} />

A Proposition represents a single unit of expression.

It could be a statement;

<PropositionCard proposition="Matter and energy might be expressions of underlying structure." />

an open question,

<PropositionCard proposition="What if dark matter is not unseen mass, but an unobserved mode of structure?" />

or a claim,

<PropositionCard proposition="Matter does not carry information, it is the manifestation of it." />

A Proposition is not valid or invalid by default.
Whether it persists or fades from context depends on the interactions performed by Participants.

A Proposition can exist of two tiers:

### Attestation

<IconWhirl size={64} />

<AttestationInput />

An Attestation is a directional participation applied to a Proposition:

- **Support**: The Participant agrees with the Proposition.
- **Refute**: The Participant disagrees with the Proposition.

Rules for Attesting:

- It is not a free action and costs Charge to perform.
- A Participant can only apply one Attestation per Proposition.
- A Participant can change their stance, but at an additional cost.
- Participants can optionally provide a rationale and attach [Receipts](#Receipt).

These rules allow an Attestation to hold significance, while reducing spam behaviour.

---

### Receipt

A Receipt is evidence generated by the system, as a result of Actions performed by Participants.

- Can be attached to Propositions and Attestations, which increases Prominence as a side-effect.
- Provides metadata results of an Action (file path location, URL, dataset, hash, etc.).
- Act as anchors for committal Propositions.
- No committal proposition can exist without a Receipt.

For a proof of concept, the following receipt types are defined:

- **Cite**: A reference to another Proposition or Attestation.
- **Artefact**: A file that lives in a configurable Data Storage location.
- **Hyperlink**: A URL, title and summary for a web page.

Receipt types can be extended as the system evolves and further utility is desired.
